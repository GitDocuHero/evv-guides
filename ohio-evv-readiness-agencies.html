Ohio EVV Readiness Guide

What changes on October 6, and what to verify before it does

For Ohio DD agencies and county boards. A working guide to Senate Bill 315, the EVV requirements already in force, and the documentation gaps that cause preventable claim denials.

Start with what is not new

Most of what is circulating about Senate Bill 315 treats October 6 as the moment EVV starts mattering for your billing. For Ohio DD providers, that is not accurate, and it is worth being precise because the misunderstanding causes agencies to prepare for the wrong thing.

EVV as a condition of payment has already applied to you since October 1, 2025. A claim that requires EVV without a matching EVV record is denied. That applies to HPC, PD-HPC, and Residential Respite delivered on the Individual Options, Level One, and SELF waivers. If your agency has been absorbing denials for the last year, that rule is why.

What October 6 does is widen the surface. It does not repair anything you already have wrong.

What Senate Bill 315 actually changes

Ohio Medicaid to maintain an EVV performance dashboardYour numbers become visible in a way they were not before.
On the live-in caregiver exemption. Senate Bill 315 exempts live-in family caregivers from EVV GPS tracking. Separately, and already in force, Ohio Administrative Code rule 5160-32-02 lets a direct care worker who lives in the same household as the individual they support request an exemption from visit logging. That is a form you can file now, not something to wait for: the EVV Exemption Request is on the Ohio Department of Medicaid site, and accepted proof of shared address includes a utility bill, a current state ID or driver's licence, a W2, or a tax return.

Two things worth putting on someone's list. Any change in live-in status has to be reported to the affiliated agency and to evv@medicaid.ohio.gov within five business days. And SB 315 also directs ODM to establish separate oversight for family caregivers, so expect further guidance rather than treating the current process as final.

Five documentation gaps that cause denials

These are the failure modes that produce EVV claim-matching denials. In most agencies the losses are not spread evenly across all five. One or two account for the majority, which is why sorting your denials by cause is worth more than a general tightening.

  1. Missing call-in or call-out

    A DSP forgets to clock out, or the app loses signal at the end of a shift. The visit is real, the note is complete, and the aggregator holds half a record. This is the most common single cause.

  2. Service code mismatch

    The code captured at the visit is not the code that gets billed. The claim is internally correct and has nothing to match against.

  3. Missing or incorrect Medicaid identifier

    One transposed digit on a client record and every visit for that person fails to match, often for months before anyone traces it back to the record rather than the visits.

  4. Service delivered outside the authorization window

    An ISP lapses or is revised mid month, staff continue delivering, and the visits fall outside what was authorized on the date of service.

  5. Records transmitted out of sequence

    Order matters. If the aggregator does not yet hold the recipient or the direct care worker, the visit is rejected. A rejected visit re-sent without an actual change is rejected again.

What to verify with Sandata before the deadline

Sandata is the state aggregator. Your records have to be loaded and accepted there before the visits referencing them can match.

Recipient and worker records load before visits

Confirm every active recipient and every direct care worker is present and accepted. A visit for someone the aggregator does not hold cannot succeed no matter how correct the visit itself is.

Rejections are being read, not just counted

A rejection carries a reason. Agencies that track a rejection count without reading the reasons tend to re-send the same records unchanged, which produces the same rejection.

Your provider identifiers are current

Medicaid provider number and NPI need to match what the state holds. These change more often than people expect, particularly after an ownership or enrollment change.

Someone owns the exception queue

Exceptions do not resolve themselves and they age badly. Whoever owns it should be named, not assumed.

Checklist to hand your staff

  1. Clock in at the location, not on the way to it

    In the home or in the community where the service is happening. Not the car, not the driveway.

  2. Clock out before leaving

    The single most common preventable failure. If the app cannot connect, note it and report it the same day rather than at the end of the pay period.

  3. Confirm the service you are delivering is the service on the plan

    If what the person needs today is not what is authorized, that is a conversation with the administrator, not a field substitution.

  4. Report a missed or broken clock event the same day

    A correction made the same week is routine. The same correction made after billing is an exception with a paper trail.

  5. Say something when a client record looks wrong

    Wrong name spelling, wrong address, wrong identifier. Field staff notice these first and are usually the last to be asked.

A reasonable sequence between now and October 6

This week. Pull sixty days of denied claims and sort them by which of the five causes applies. Identify which one or two are doing the most damage.

Next. Walk one shift end to end and watch where your DSP is physically standing at clock-in and clock-out. That single observation predicts most of your exception volume.

Then. Reconcile ISP authorization dates against what staff are actually delivering, especially anywhere an ISP was revised mid month. Confirm recipient and worker records are loaded and accepted at the aggregator.

Before the deadline. If you touch nonemergency medical transportation, read the rule rather than waiting to see how it lands. Where your program is genuinely ambiguous, ask ODM in writing. A written answer before October 6 is worth considerably more than a phone call after it.
